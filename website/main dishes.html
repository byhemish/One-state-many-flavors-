Bisi Bele Bath

Bisi Bele Bath is a comforting, traditional Karnataka dish ("hot lentil rice") made with rice, lentils, vegetables, and a rich blend of aromatic spices and tangy tamarind.

Prep: 15 minutes Cook: 55 minutes Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup rice
  • ½ cup toor dal
  • 2 cups mixed vegetables (carrot, beans, potato)
  • 3 tbsp bisi bele bath powder
  • 1 tsp tamarind paste
  • Salt to taste
  • 2 tbsp ghee
  • Mustard seeds, curry leaves (for tempering)

Instructions

  1. Pressure cook rice and dal together until soft. Mash lightly.
  2. Boil vegetables in water with turmeric and salt until tender.
  3. Add cooked rice-dal mixture, bisi bele bath powder, and tamarind paste to vegetables.
  4. Simmer for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking.
  5. Temper mustard seeds and curry leaves in ghee. Pour over the dish.
  6. Serve hot with papad or raita.

Ragi Mudde

Ragi Mudde is a traditional Karnataka staple made from wholesome finger millet flour (ragi). These nutritious, soft balls are a classic rural comfort food, traditionally paired with spicy saaru, sambar, or rich meat curries.

Prep: 10 minutes Cook: 20 minutes Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 2 cups ragi flour (finger millet)
  • 4 cups water
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. Boil 3 cups water in a heavy-bottomed vessel.
  2. Mix ragi flour with 1 cup cold water to make a smooth slurry (no lumps).
  3. Pour slurry into boiling water while stirring continuously.
  4. Cook on low heat for 5-7 minutes, stirring constantly until it forms a thick dough.
  5. Wet your hands, take a portion of dough, and roll into a smooth ball.
  6. Serve immediately with saaru, sambar, or curry.

Vangi Bath

Vangi Bath is a classic Karnataka spiced rice dish cooked with tender brinjal (eggplant), roasted aromatic spices, and tangy tamarind, popular for everyday meals and family gatherings.

Prep: 15 minutes Cook: 30 minutes Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 2 cups rice (cooked and cooled)
  • 2 medium brinjals, cubed
  • 3 tbsp vangi bath spice powder
  • 1 tsp tamarind paste
  • 1 tsp jaggery
  • Mustard seeds, curry leaves, dried red chillies
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. Heat oil, temper mustard seeds, curry leaves, and dried red chillies.
  2. Add brinjal cubes and sauté until half-cooked.
  3. Add vangi bath powder, tamarind paste, jaggery, and salt. Mix well.
  4. Cook until brinjal is fully tender and masala is fragrant.
  5. Add cooked rice and gently mix to coat evenly without breaking grains.
  6. Serve warm with raita or papad.

Kori Gassi

Kori Gassi is a rich, aromatic Mangalorean chicken curry featuring a creamy coconut base, roasted coastal spices, and gentle heat, traditionally paired with neer dosa or rice.

Prep: 25 minutes Cook: 35 minutes Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 500g chicken, cut into pieces
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 4-5 dried red chillies
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • ½ tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 inch ginger, 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • Curry leaves, salt
  • 2 tbsp oil

Instructions

  1. Dry roast coconut, red chillies, coriander, fenugreek, ginger, and garlic until fragrant.
  2. Cool and grind to a smooth paste with little water.
  3. Heat oil, sauté onions and curry leaves until golden.
  4. Add chicken pieces and cook until sealed on all sides.
  5. Add ground masala paste and 1 cup water. Simmer covered for 20-25 minutes.
  6. Adjust salt and consistency. Serve hot with neer dosa or steamed rice.

Mangalorean Fish Curry

Mangalorean Fish Curry is a vibrant coastal Karnataka seafood dish featuring a fiery, tangy gravy crafted from fresh coconut and tamarind.

Prep: 25 minutes Cook: 30 minutes Serves: 4

Ingredients

  • 500g fish fillets (kingfish or pomfret)
  • 1 cup grated coconut
  • 6-8 dried red chillies
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • ½ tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 tsp tamarind paste
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • Curry leaves, salt
  • 2 tbsp oil

Instructions

  1. Grind coconut, red chillies, coriander, and fenugreek into a fine paste.
  2. Heat oil, sauté onions and curry leaves until soft.
  3. Add ground masala and cook for 3-4 minutes until raw smell disappears.
  4. Add 2 cups water, tamarind paste, and salt. Bring to a gentle boil.
  5. Slide in fish pieces carefully. Cook on low heat for 8-10 minutes (do not stir vigorously).
  6. Serve hot with steamed rice or neer dosa.
